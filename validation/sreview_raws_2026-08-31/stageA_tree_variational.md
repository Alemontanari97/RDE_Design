## PART 0 — FOUR DRY LEMMAS THAT SHAPE EVERYTHING BELOW
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

### L0.1 (THEOREM under the pure-rotating-wave pin). Thrust is STEADY; the time mean is exact and needs no limit.
Hypotheses: (H1) the lab-frame field is a rigidly rotating pattern q(x,r,θ,t) = Q(x,r,θ−Ω_p t) with Q 2π-periodic
in θ (this is exactly the pinned data family propagated through a FIXED AXISYMMETRIC nozzle: the domain and the
boundary operators are θ-invariant, so if a solution of the declared class exists for the rotating data it is a
rotating pattern, up to the non-uniqueness caveat of SP2); (H2) the control surface Σ is axisymmetric.
Claim: F_Σ(t) = ∫_Σ [ρ u_x (u·n) + (p−Pa) n_x] dA = ∫∫ Φ(Q(x,r,θ−Ω_p t)) r dθ ds is INDEPENDENT of t (shift
θ → θ+Ω_p t under the full-circle integral). Hence J_true[S] = F_Σ exactly; liminf/limsup variants collapse;
J_true equals the azimuthal integral of the axial-momentum flux of the STEADY field Q in the frame rotating at Ω_p.
Corollary (side loads, THEOREM): only the first azimuthal harmonic of Q contributes to a net lateral force; it is a
vector of constant magnitude rotating at Ω_p; for n ≥ 2 waves with n-fold symmetry of Q the net lateral force
vanishes identically. Falsifier: a computed rotating-frame steady field whose axisymmetric-surface thrust
integral varies with the surface's azimuthal phase (numerical), or data whose spectrum has energy outside the
harmonics of f (pin violation, SP7 audit).
Consequence: the unsteady objective (O-a) is a STEADY 3D shape-optimization objective in the rotating frame. The
"unsteadiness/reduction error" of (R-iii) is entirely the 3D-vs-reduced-representation error of SP5.

### L0.2 (THEOREM, elementary). The two Strouhal limits are axisymmetric steady problems, both computable
with steady axisymmetric tools; the finite-St answer is NOT sandwiched by them in general.
(i) St → 0 (quasi-steady): at each azimuth φ the flow is the steady axisymmetric solution for the interface state
at phase φ; J = ∫_0^{2π} J_steady(S; data(φ)) dφ/2π (a MULTIPOINT objective, weights = azimuthal measure).
(ii) St → ∞ (homogenized): the nozzle sees a steady axisymmetric inflow whose FLUXES (mass, momentum, energy,
species handle) are the azimuthal averages of the data fluxes; the "equivalent state" is the solution of the
flux-inversion problem (unique on the supersonic branch when the averaged fluxes are realizable; realizability
must be checked — non-realizable averaged fluxes are a loud reject). Mixing entropy is generated by the
averaging: J_hom ≤ the ideal bound of L0.3, strictly when the data are non-uniform.
(iii) No order between J(St), J_qs and J_hom holds in general (nonlinear flux; counterexample by a phase-dependent
shock position). Hence the two limits give a DIAGNOSTIC pair, not a certified bracket; the finite-St value must be
MEASURED (SP5 protocol: f is a parameter, so an Ω_p-sweep of the rotating-frame solver at fixed data gives J(St)
between the two limits it must approach — a rejectable convergence test in both directions).
Falsifier: the Ω_p-sweep fails to converge to J_qs as Ω_p→0 or to J_hom as Ω_p→∞ beyond the discretization band.

### L0.3 (THEOREM*, closure: attached inviscid flow; ideal bound). For any admissible S, J[S] ≤ J_ideal(data) :=
∫ over Γ_d of the axial momentum flux obtained by expanding each interface stream tube isentropically and
without turning losses to Pa (per phase, then azimuthally averaged). Proof sketch: thrust = axial momentum flux
at any downstream axisymmetric surface + pressure term; entropy is non-decreasing along particle paths (Lax
condition across captured discontinuities); the axial momentum for a given stagnation state and entropy is
maximized by axial, fully expanded exit; over-expansion and non-axial exit both lose. Rigor gap: mixing between
tubes (slip surfaces) can transfer axial momentum between streams but not increase the total available work;
the bound holds for the sum (SCHEMA at the tube-to-tube level, THEOREM* for the total under the availability
argument).
Use: (R-i) "within computed gap δ = (J_ideal − J[S*])/J_ideal of the best possible" is a CHECKABLE global
statement for every road; and the NEGATIVE answer to Q0 is a one-liner if J_practice ≥ J_ideal − m.

### L0.4 (LEMMA, obstruction to a "cycle-averaged Rao"). Rao's method [KNOWLEDGE: Rao 1958, full] turns the
contour problem into an isoperimetric problem on a control surface = the last left-running characteristic
through the lip; the exit-angle and Mach conditions along that curve are algebraic because the objective and the
mass constraint are integrals along ONE curve. For a multipoint objective with phase-dependent inflow Mach
number, the last characteristics of different phases are DIFFERENT curves; no common control surface exists,
so the isoperimetric reduction fails (except when all phases share the characteristic net, which the varying
Mach number excludes). Consequence: the multipoint problem needs a general adjoint (SP3), and Rao stays the
COMPARATOR (SP-CARM), not the method.

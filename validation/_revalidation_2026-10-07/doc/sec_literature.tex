\section{Literature anchor: Rao 1961, Humphreys et al.\ 1971 (18/20)}\label{s:lit}
The optimiser cases take GENO's Rao contour as the optimum; here that contour is tied to the papers. Rao's optimum
carries, along its exit characteristic $ED$ (a $C^-$ from the lip $E$ to the plug end $D$),
$w\cos(\theta+\alpha)/\cos\alpha=C_2$ and $R\rho w^2\sin^2\theta\tan\alpha=C_3$, with $(p-p_{a|b})\cot\alpha/(\tfrac12\rho
w^2)=-\sin2\theta$ at $E$ (giving $p_a$) and at $D$ (ending $ED$). We solve these from the paper alone
(\texttt{ref/rao1961.py}; the result is an exact $C^-$ to $10^{-15}$); GENO is given Rao's lip state.

\verdict{\yes. Rao's tables are the exact solution of his own equations ended at his printed $R_D$ ($L$, $\varepsilon$,
$C_F$ to the printed digits; GENO the same); his end point corresponds to a base pressure of $0.0033\,P_c$ instead of
$0$, i.e.\ his march stopped about one step early. The printed contours lie on GENO's within $8\times10^{-4}R_E$ (31
points; 3-decimal printing allows $7\times10^{-4}$). The two misses are the paper's: $p_a/P_c$ is printed $0.0355$ for
$0.035565$ (truncated), and $\theta_D$ at his end point is $0.043^\circ$ off for a printing tolerance of $0.037^\circ$.}

\textbf{Humphreys et al.\ 1971} (their Rao nozzle: 148.08 lbm/s, end at 11.518 in from the lip, $p_a=14.7$ psia,
$P_c=500$ psia, $\gamma=1.23$, base $0.846\,p_D/M_D^{1.3}$): our solution gives $R_E=8.3311$ in (printed 8.33),
$R_D=1.37507$ in (1.37506), thrust 34{,}254 lbf (34{,}253); GENO lies within 0.0003 in of their 19-point contour (their
$-12.27^\circ$ at $x=5.337$ in is a misprint for $-22.27^\circ$: GENO $-22.29^\circ$). Their main result is the premise of
\S\ref{s:opt}: a direct optimiser started on a characteristic of Rao's own field re-obtains Rao's thrust (34{,}373 vs
34{,}375 lbf); their fixed-inlet optimum is not reproducible (start line not published, worth 8\,\% of thrust).

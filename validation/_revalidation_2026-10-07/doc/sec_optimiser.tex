\section{The optimiser against known optima}\label{s:opt}
The driver is \texttt{run\_trsqp} with the production switches (backtracking 4, objective scaled by the radius, radius
follows the step). Each case has an optimum known independently of the model, and is run from at least two starts.

\textbf{The bound.} On a plane downstream of any shock-free plug with the jet edge and the base at $p_a$, the thrust is
$T=\int(\rho u^2+p-p_a)\,dA$. Per unit mass, with $u=V\cos\theta\le V$,
\begin{equation}
u+\frac{p-p_a}{\rho u}\le V+\frac{p-p_a}{\rho V}\le V_a,\qquad
\text{since}\ \ \frac{d}{dp}\Big[V+\frac{p-p_a}{\rho V}\Big]=-\frac{p-p_a}{(\rho V)^2}\,\frac{d(\rho V)}{dp}\ \ \text{and}\ \ \frac{d(\rho V)}{dp}>0\ \text{(supersonic)},
\end{equation}
so $T\le\dot mV_a$, with equality only for a uniform axial exit at $p_a$ (the ideal plug). In vacuum with a fixed exit
area the same argument gives $F_\mathrm{1D}$ (the perfect nozzle). On a fixed inflow and a fixed end point, Rao's
contour is the optimum (Humphreys et al.\ 1971 confirm it numerically).

\subsection{Planar plug: the exact ideal plug (9/11)}\label{s:planar}
The planar flow of \S\ref{s:t03}; the plug is a cubic spline through 12 knots; optimum $T=\dot mV_a$.
\begin{center}\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{../figs/11_profiles_planar.png}\end{center}
\verdict{\yes, up to the basis. The two distant starts (the ideal plug of a $15^\circ$ fan, $1.6\times10^{-3}$ below;
a straight line, $3\times10^{-2}$ below) land on one contour ($1.2\times10^{-4}$) and one value ($3\times10^{-10}$),
$9.1\times10^{-6}$ below the bound; the start near the ideal, run with a 16 times coarser radius floor, stops
$2.6\times10^{-7}$ lower. That gap is the spline's: the ideal plug's curvature jumps where the fan's first wave lands ($x=0.666$), a
$C^2$ spline cannot, its wall-angle error is $\delta\theta_\mathrm{rms}=2.1\times10^{-3}$ rad, and at a maximum
$\Delta T/T\approx-2\,\delta\theta_\mathrm{rms}^2=-9\times10^{-6}$. The march itself converges to $\dot mV_a$ on the exact
ideal contour ($+1.7\times10^{-7}$ at 320 stations).}
\textbf{With the no-fold class on: no.} From a start inside the class the walk stalls at $-4.9\times10^{-5}$ while the
class is far from active (margin $+0.59$): scipy's interior-point segment returns a point worse than its start for every
barrier and scaling tried (defect 3, \S\ref{s:defects}); from a start outside the class there is no step at all
(defect 2).

\subsection{The production walker on the same nozzle}\label{s:walker}
The production walker (\texttt{TwoWall.walk()}, what the launch scripts run) picks its start, reads the radius from the
class scale along the start's gradient (a ladder of trial steps), builds a Newton metric from a secant Hessian
(\texttt{TWOP\_METRIC=start}), and calls \texttt{run\_trsqp} in those coordinates with the no-fold class on
(production constants). Posing and optimum as in \S\ref{s:migdal}.
\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{lccc}\toprule
start & records & landing $C_F$ & distance to \S\ref{s:migdal}'s landing\\\midrule
GENO's walls + 0.2\,\% bump (in class) & 3 & 1.647643905 & $1.5\times10^{-5}$ (knots)\\
straight walls, ramped toward GENO's & --- & nothing to walk & ---\\\bottomrule
\end{tabular}\end{center}
\verdict{\yes\ from an in-class start: the walker lands on the point of \S\ref{s:migdal} ($C_F$ equal to 9 digits,
$|\nabla J|_\infty=2\times10^{-8}$, in class) in 3 records instead of 11--13 (259 s in all against 485 s, although its
own overhead --- an 11-step radius ladder and a 62 s secant Hessian --- is included): the metric from the start's
Hessian (all 22 eigenvalues of $-H$ positive, none floored) makes each segment a near-Newton step. Its start is only
$2\times10^{-3}$ from GENO's walls: on this posing the class admits only near starts. \no\ from the generic start:
the straight walls and every ramp toward GENO's walls fold the channel (KS $-0.68$ already at 25\,\%), and the walker's
own restoration exists only for capped posings, so it stops with ``nothing to walk'' (defect 2).}

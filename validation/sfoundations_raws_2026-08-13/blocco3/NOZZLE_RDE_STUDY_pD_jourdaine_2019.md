## (1) IDENTITY + METHOD SUMMARY (pages in parentheses = printed pages)

- SOLVER: 3-D compressible Euler, 9 species (H2, O2, H, O, OH, HO2,
  H2O2, H2O, N2) mass conservation, explicit 3rd-order TVD
  Runge-Kutta time integration; convective flux = 2nd-order AUSMDV
  [ref 25]. Viscosity, thermal and mass diffusion NEGLECTED
  (explicitly assumed negligible) (p. 3444, §2.1).
- CHEMISTRY: detailed kinetics, UT-JAXA model [ref 24], 21 elementary
  reactions; stiffness avoided via linearly point-implicit source
  terms (p. 3444, §2.1).
- GEOMETRY (Fig. 1, p. 3444): annular combustion chamber + CONIC
  aerospike nozzle, two configurations: OPEN and CHOKED. H2/O2
  physical domain is MICRO-SCALE: total length 2.252 mm, chamber
  section 1.5 mm, channel width 0.877 mm (0.7 mm at the choked
  convergent), spike half-angle 50 deg. H2/Air domain = the same
  geometry multiplied by 13.3 (p. 3444, §2.2 + Fig. 1). Choked config
  = addition of a convergent zone reducing the channel exit area by a
  factor 0.8; area ratio Ae/At = 2.77 (open) and 3.47 (choked)
  (p. 3444, §2.2).
- INJECTION/BCs: premixed idealized injection through micro-nozzles,
  area ratio A*/A = 0.2; mostly-supersonic inlet with subsonic
  patches where the detonation circulates near the micro-nozzle
  exits; two-BC, four-step injection management per Zhdan et al.
  [ref 4] (p. 3444, §2.2-2.3; Fig. 2 p. 3445). Ambient behind the
  aerospike: 0.1 MPa, 300 K (p. 3445, §2.3).
- GRID: three connected zones (Fig. 3, p. 3445), interpolation
  coefficients connecting them; Table 1 (p. 3445) gives per-zone
  dimensions (axial x radial x circumferential), e.g. H2/O2 open
  zone 1 = 63x21x601, min widths 5 um axial / 4.4 um radial / 3.7 um
  circumferential; choked zone 2 = 266x31x151. Zone-1 refinement test
  63x21x601 -> 63x21x1201 (p. 3446, §3.1).
- CASES (Table 2, p. 3446): H2/O2 open+choked, micro-nozzle
  stagnation pressure P0 = 2-5 MPa, Phi = 1; H2/Air open+choked,
  P0 = 3 MPa, Phi = 0.8-1.4.

## (2) FINDINGS LEDGER (every result/consideration; ADVISORY class)

F-1 (p. 3446, §3.1): the simulation stabilizes ONE detonation wave
  regardless of mixture equivalence ratio and mass flow rate —
  "in contrary with experiments". Two-wave initialization: after 15
  cycles (fine grid) only one detonation remained; mechanisms of wave
  creation/disappearance and grid-resolution effects on wave-count
  stability declared NOT fully understood by the authors.
F-2 (p. 3446, §3.1): a completed 2-wave simulation showed NO
  performance difference vs 1 wave even after 68 detonation cycles,
  though mass-flow-rate oscillations appear. (Contrast: their ref
  [27] Tsuboi 2017 reports 10% Isp reduction with two waves.)
F-3 (Table 3, p. 3446): detonation velocity 95-99% of CJ across all
  cases (H2/O2 open 2693-2827 m/s = 96-99% CJ; choked 2781-2907 =
  95-97%; H2/Air open 1853-2057 = 95%; choked 1856-1994 = 97%);
  closeness attributed to idealized premixed injection; wall
  curvature explains the residual deficit.
F-4 (p. 3446, §3.1): OPEN config — gas expands downstream,
  principally sonic or supersonic before entering the nozzle.
F-5 (p. 3446, §3.1): CHOKED config — flow in the chamber is
  ESSENTIALLY SUBSONIC due to the throat at chamber exit; the oblique
  shock generated by the detonation REFLECTS on the throat and
  PROGRESSES BACKWARD (upstream) into the engine.
F-6 (Fig. 4 + p. 3446, §3.1): TIME-AVERAGED exhaust flow (P0 = 3 MPa,
  H2/O2): "relatively the same for both configurations";
  under-expanded; expansion fan at the detonation-chamber exit, free
  jet boundary + compression fan, inward turn creating a shock triple
  point and high-pressure area, then re-expansion — "typical of a
  shock under-expanded supersonic diamonds".
F-7 (p. 3446, §3.2, Eqs. (1)-(3)): thrust bookkeeping for a complete
  aerospike: F_exit = mdot·V_exit + (P_exit - P_inf)·A_exit;
  F_ramp = ∫(P_ramp - P_inf) dA_ramp (spike surface projected on the
  radial plane); F = F_ramp + F_exit; Isp = F/(mdot·g0) with F the
  CYCLE-AVERAGE thrust (Eq. (4)).
F-8 (Fig. 5 + p. 3446-3447, §3.2, H2/O2): mass flux 495 -> 1260
  kg/s/m2 raises Isp by 30.8 s (open) and 22.4 s (choked); at given
  mass flow and Phi the CHOKED aerospike improves Isp by 4-7% over
  open; Isp anchors: open 237 -> 268 s, choked 256 -> 279 s (P0 = 2
  -> 5 MPa, dashed boxes in Fig. 5).
F-9 (p. 3446, §3.2): mechanism claim: higher chamber pressure (from
  raising mass flow or decreasing throat area) adds pressure thrust
  through the nozzle — "explained through the isotropic [sic,
  isentropic] flow theory".
F-10 (p. 3447, §3.2): ideal-rocket comparison, Gordon-McBride CEA
  (infinite chamber area, chemical equilibrium, atmospheric
  environment): idealized Isp 287-307 s at fixed area ratio — the
  simulated RDE Isp stays UNDER the idealized classic rocket values
  for the same configuration.
F-11 (Figs. 6-7, p. 3448 + p. 3447, §3.2, H2/Air, mass flux 1010+/-40
  kg/s/m2, Phi 0.8-1.4): specific thrust INCREASES with Phi; Isp
  (fuel-based) is higher at LOW Phi; choked outperforms open
  regardless of Phi; curve trends "agree well with the experiments by
  Fotia et al. [23]" (plots show simulation consistently ABOVE the
  experimental points with error bars).
F-12 (p. 3447, §3.2): the authors' own gap admission: "A gap
  separates the values obtained by the simulations and the
  experiments", attributed to the oversimplified numerical model — a
  realistic simulation should include detonation-wave-number
  variation, multi-port injection, mixture non-homogeneity,
  viscosity, non-adiabatic walls.
F-13 (p. 3447, §3.3): pressure-gain accounting: difficulty = defining
  chamber pressure Pc in the unsteady RDE environment; their choice:
  Pc = STATIC TIME-AVERAGED pressure in the chamber, averaged
  downstream of the detonation wave and at the chamber exit.
F-14 (p. 3447, §3.3 + abstract p. 3443): CHOKED nozzle raises the
  chamber time-averaged static pressure by ~50% (H2/O2) and ~60%
  (H2/Air) vs open — the paper's headline "pressure gain" is CHAMBER
  PRESSURIZATION BY CHOKING, not an exit-total-pressure gain vs a
  deflagration baseline (definitional point for the synthesis).
F-15 (Table 4, p. 3448): H2/Air time-average Pc across Phi = 0.8-1.4:
  open 0.83-0.88 MPa; choked 1.35-1.40 MPa.
F-16 (Fig. 8 + p. 3448): axial time-average static pressure: three
  zones (detonation zone / downstream expansion / fast expansion at
  nozzle start); choked cases hold a pressure PLATEAU up to the
  throat then drop sharply; a COMPRESSION ZONE around x = 1 mm
  (H2/O2) and x = 12 mm (H2/Air) in the choked config = interaction
  of the oblique shock with the shock reflected at the throat
  propagating upstream in the channel (Fig. 9 shows it).
F-17 (p. 3449, §3.4, Eq. (5)): thrust coefficient Cf = F/(At·Pi),
  Pi = combustion-chamber stagnation pressure; the authors state the
  DEFINITIONAL PROBLEM: "it is difficult to justly define the chamber
  pressure in a detonation chamber with a broad range of pressure
  distribution".
F-18 (p. 3449, §3.4, Eq. (6)): c* = At·Pi/mdot = Isp·g0/Cf; c*
  efficiency eta = c*_RDE/c*_ideal x 100, ideal c* from CEA (CJ
  detonation problem; fresh-gas conditions upstream of the wave;
  ideal Ti assumed constant in the chamber; time-averaged stagnation
  values used) (p. 3450).
F-19 (p. 3450, §3.4, H2/O2): Cf = 1.00 and 1.07 (open, P0 = 2 and 5
  MPa); 1.18 and 1.21 (choked). Authors: values "particularly small",
  the open nozzle "almost does not produce any additional thrust";
  "Both nozzles have a simple geometry and are NOT optimized
  resulting in these poor performances".
F-20 (Fig. 10, p. 3450): c* efficiency: H2/O2 open 86.1-86.9%, choked
  88.5-88.8% (vs mass flux); H2/Air open 79.3-81.0%, choked
  83.5-85.0% (vs Phi), peak at Phi = 1 (highest stagnation
  temperature); efficiency drops lean/rich via weaker detonation +
  unburned gas; ideal stagnation temperatures are ~14-19% larger than
  simulated.
F-21 (Table 5, p. 3450, H2/Air): open F = 60.7-62.2 N, Pi = 1.39-1.46
  MPa, Cf = 1.08-1.11; choked F = 63.8-65.4 N, Pi = 1.73-1.79 MPa,
  Cf = 1.16-1.18; highest Cf at lean mixture, Cf decreases as Phi
  increases (stagnation pressure and thrust ratio drop continuously
  lean -> rich).
F-22 (p. 3450, Conclusions): "The time-averaged exhaust flow is
  similar to a classic axis-symmetric steady exhaust flow suggesting
  that classic design method for aerospike nozzle could be used with
  RDE." (THE bearing sentence of the paper for our program.)
F-23 (p. 3450-3451, Conclusions): choked aerospike globally improves
  performance: chamber static pressure gain 50%/60%, Isp ~5% higher,
  chamber c* efficiency +~2% (H2/O2) / +~4% (H2/Air); triple points
  from the shock interactions "may create high heat flux on the
  wall".
F-24 (p. 3451, Conclusions): the paper's own evidence-class caveat,
  verbatim: "The trend of Isp and specific thrust are close to the
  experiments, however, THE ABSOLUTE VALUES ARE COMPLETELY DIFFERENT
  since the mathematical model is simple and the RDE domains are not
  at scale." (Micro-scale mm-domain; premixed inviscid model.)
F-25 (design-method step census, whole paper): the paper contains NO
  nozzle DESIGN METHOD — the conic spike is a fixed 50-deg geometry,
  explicitly non-optimized (F-19); the paper's method content is the
  performance-evaluation chain (Eqs. (1)-(6)): cycle-averaged thrust
  decomposition -> Isp -> Cf -> c* efficiency vs CEA ideal, plus the
  Pc-definition choice (F-13, F-17). No MoC, no truncation study, no
  swirl/deflection statistics are reported anywhere in the paper.

## 3. What the paper actually does

**Problem.** Design of **two-dimensional (planar, ν = 0)** altitude-compensating **plug (spike)** nozzles for a Reusable Launch Vehicle, providing **maximum thrust in vacuum** at given plug length X and "half maximal cross section" Y (relative length L = X/Y), with the plug **optimally conjoined either to a base or to the contour of turbopump nozzles (turbonozzles, TN)** that replace part of the base. Second, separate task: **evaluation** (not design) of the thrust of those configurations **at start** (p^e = 1 atm), accounting for boundary-layer separation.

**Formulation.** Classical **control contour method (CCM)** of the Russian school, in the author's own repaired variant **ICCM** ("indefinite control contour method", Ref. 5 = Kraiko 1979). The 2-D shape problem for the plug contour *ab* is reduced to a **one-dimensional variational problem for the optimal control characteristic *cb*** (C⁻ family for the external/plug problem, C⁺ for the internal/bell problem); the contour is then recovered as a **streamline of the corresponding Gursat problem** (p. 1347, col. 2).

**Unknowns.** (i) the parameter distribution (V, θ) on the control characteristic *cb* — which for the **plane 2-D case collapses to CONSTANT parameters on cb** ("it was established that flow parameters are constant on cb for a plane two-dimensional nozzle", p. 1348 col. 1; re-asserted on p. 1349 col. 2: "According to this solution, maximal thrust is provided by constant parameters on cb"); (ii) the endpoint ordinate y_b, fixed by the **Busemann condition**; (iii) the location of point c on the initial rarefaction fan; (iv) in the joint plug+TN case, the two endpoint ordinates y_{b+}, y_{b−}. **Effective DOF is O(1)–O(5) scalars**, because optimality is built into the construction.

**Constraints.** Given X (supersonic-section length) and Y (endpoint ordinate / half maximal cross section) — or, alternatively, external pressure p⁺ instead of Y; a bound on contour curvature ("a section of maximal acceptable (in absolute value) curvature", p. 1349 col. 1); base condition y_b ≤ y_g = Y; primary-nozzle exit state fixed (k₀ = 1.2, p_st0 = 160 atm, θ = θ₀ = −45 deg, M = M₀ = 2.5); turbonozzle gas separately specified (k_t = 1.37, (p_st)_t/p_st0 = 0.0158, (RT_st)_t/(RT_st0) = 1.179, m_t/m = 0.0417) — i.e. **two different gases on the same optimized wall, separated by a slip line**.

**Flow model.** Steady, inviscid, supersonic, planar; perfect gas in the computations; the underlying theory is stated for an **arbitrary two-parametric gas** and, for the second first integral, also for **rotational (non-uniform stagnation parameters)** flow. Viscosity enters only in the off-design evaluation, through an empirical separation criterion.

**Solver.**
- *Design*: boundary-value problem for ODEs on *cb* with conditions at b and c (Ref. 7 reduction), closed in finite form by the two first integrals Eq. (2)/(3); endpoint ordinate by root-finding on the Busemann condition Eq. (1); contour by MOC/Gursat.
- *Off-design (start)*: initial supersonic section i i° computed at p^e = 1 atm by the **method of characteristics**; then a **monotone shock-capturing marching scheme** (Ref. 37 = Godunov, Zabrodin, Ivanov, Kraiko, Prokopov 1976). Explicit numerical choice of record (p. 1349 col. 2): *"The authors considered the second-order approximation; however, the use of the first-order scheme for two grids with consequent **Richardson extrapolation on zero step** proved to be more convenient."*
- *Separation*: empirical critical pressure ratio, **Eq. (4)**, `p_cr/p = 1 + 0.2 k M² / (M² − 1)^{1/4}` (as printed; M, p are the values before the shock; Ref. 38 = Abramovich, *Applied Gasdynamics*, Part 1, 1991), plus an experimental correlation for the nondimensional "separation distance" (x_o − x_e)/δ*_o (Refs. 38–40).

**Verification.** No experimental comparison, no grid-convergence table for the design. Verification is (a) internal: thrust R and R_Σ compared against two ideal references R^{id1}, R^{id2} with loss percentages ΔR₁, ΔR₂ (Tables 1 and 2); (b) an explicit **insensitivity study** on the one uncertain input: thrust recomputed for separation-point positions x_e = 75, 80, 85, 90 against the attached-flow reflection point x_o ≈ 92, with the verdict *"the thrusts values R and R_Σ are practically independent from the separation zone's size"* (p. 1350) even though *"the choice of the point e position on the plug has a great influence on the distribution of p"*.

**Headline results.** L = 2.7: plug adjoins the TN **without a base** (Table 1, y_b ≈ 10.8/9.7/9.1/7.4; R ≈ 2.86 in vacuum, R ≈ 2.45 on Earth, thrusts scaled by ρ_* a_*² h, given for half the device). L ≈ 1.35 (RLV short plug): a **big base** appears, X₁ = 368/Y₁ = 272, X₂ = 279/Y₂ = 207, X₃ = 233/Y₃ = 173; optimal relative base sizes for p⁺ = 0 are H₁ = 0.47, H₂ = 0.44, H₃ = 0.43 (H scaled by Y), against H = 0.37 actually used in the RLV. Threshold statement: the optimal plug has a base when **L < 270/112 = 2.4** (p⁺ = 0). Conclusion of record: *"At start the thrust losses of the plug nozzles are three to four times smaller than those of the axisymmetrical 'bell' nozzles with the same expansion ratio"* — quantified in the body as **from 11% to 3%**.

## 4. Hypotheses

### Declared
1. Steady, inviscid, supersonic **planar (ν = 0)** flow on the control contour; transfer to a **characteristic** control contour is admissible (CCM/ICCM).
2. Perfect gas with constant k in the computations (k₀ = 1.2, k_t = 1.37). Theory stated for an arbitrary two-parametric gas (Sternin, Ref. 12; Kraiko, Ref. 19).
3. **Irrotational (homogeneous stagnation parameters)** flow for the *first* expression of Eqs. (2)/(3): *"The first expression holds true only for irrotational flows"* (p. 1349 col. 1). The second expression is claimed to survive for arbitrary two-parametric gas **and** rotational flow (Ref. 19).
4. Objective = thrust **in vacuum** (design); p^e = 1 atm only for post-hoc evaluation.
5. **Base pressure p⁺ is given and independent of the contour ab**: *"Included in BC the base pressure p⁺, acting on the base, is supposed to be given and independent from the front of the contour ab"* (p. 1348 col. 2). Acknowledged as false in general for a base overflowed on both sides (p. 1349 col. 2) and hence eliminated in the no-base joint plug+TN case.
6. Turbulent boundary layer at separation; the empirical Eq. (4) is asserted to be **no less reliable than RANS closures**: *"Such closure equations, however, are actually not more reliable than formula (4) for separations caused by interaction of shocks with a turbulent boundary layer."*
7. Separated zone modeled as the region under the streamline ehg bounded by the suspended shock tf.

### Not declared but necessary
8. **Existence, uniqueness and admissibility** of the Gursat/BVP solution on *cb* and of the recovered streamline (no self-intersection, monotone, physically realizable) — never argued.
9. **Necessary conditions treated as sufficient**: no second-order condition, no reduced Hessian, no globality argument, no bound on the optimality gap anywhere in the paper.
10. The **slip line** between primary gas (k = 1.2) and turbopump gas (k = 1.37) is stable and resolvable, and the two-gas control contour is a legitimate single variational object.
11. p⁺ "known and determined from the plug and TN contours shapes" (p. 1349 col. 2) is **circular** in general; the authors avoid it only in the no-base configuration.
12. Boundary-layer displacement outside the separated zone is negligible for thrust (asserted, defended only via the x_e insensitivity study).
13. Eq. (4)'s empirical validity is assumed to cover the encountered Mach range (Fig. 4 colorbar spans M ≈ 2.69–3.94).
14. **Comparator asymmetry, unflagged**: the "three to four times smaller losses" verdict compares a **two-dimensional** plug against **axisymmetric** bell nozzles "with the same expansion ratio". Same-ε across different dimensionality is not a fair twin in our sense.
15. The two Tables' loss percentages are computed against ideal references whose construction (R^{id1}, R^{id2}) is described only obliquely ("thrusts of ideal flow ... and the losses of R_Σ in percents (ΔR), corresponding to gas expansion up to external pressure (or area Y), are marked with index 1 (2)").

**F-1 — CONTAINED, exact identity (ALTA).** Eq. (1), p. 1348 col. 1, the **"Busemann condition" (BC)**:
`P^±_{b±} − p⁺ = 0`, `P^± = p ± ρV² tan α sin θ cos θ = p(1 ± κM²/(2√(M²−1)) sin 2θ)`
is **algebraically identical to our CSTR_PA / CSTR_PB**. Hand check: ρV² tan α sin θ cos θ = ½ρV² sin 2θ tan α, so P^− = p⁺ reads `(p − p⁺) − ½ρV² sin 2θ tan α = 0` — verbatim our Rao-Eq.(14) form with p_a → p⁺; and with tan α = 1/√(M²−1), ρV² = κpM², the second expression follows exactly, so the printed perfect-gas form is the γ = const specialization, not an independent statement. Three consequences of record: (i) the classical **name** of our endpoint transversality is the *Busemann condition*, attributed here to Ref. 7 (Guderley & Hantsch 1955) — *"This condition for determining y_b is named as Busemann's condition (BC) in Ref. 7"*; (ii) the **sign convention is exactly ours** — *"Upper (lower) signs and indexes correspond to the internal (external) problem"*, i.e. minus for bell/shroud, plus for plug, matching "the '+'-sign mirror with base pressure p_b"; (iii) **direct support for claim 12/E4**: the general form `P^± = p ± ρV² tan α sin θ cos θ` contains only primitive variables (p, ρ, V, α), κ appearing only in the derived M-form — the endpoint condition is EOS-general and the γ = const dependence is an *expression* choice, exactly our E4 thesis.

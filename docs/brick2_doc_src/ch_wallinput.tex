\chapter{The wall becomes an input}
\label{ch:wallinput}

So far the solver has drawn its own nozzle wall: the shape came out of
the computation, and the only way to change it was to change the few
knobs that steer the construction. An optimizer that is to explore
free-form shapes needs the data flow reversed --- hand it any wall,
get the flow on that wall. This chapter performs that inversion using
the same verified building blocks, checks the new engine against the
old one on the one wall both can handle, and certifies the derivative
of thrust with respect to an arbitrary wall deformation. That
derivative is what every later optimization in this document steers by.

\section{The question}

Brick~1's march \emph{draws} its wall: the contour is computed as a
\emph{streamline} --- a line everywhere tangent to the local velocity,
the path a gas particle follows --- chosen so that exactly the throat's
mass flow passes inside it. In other words the wall is an output.
Free-form design needs the opposite: hand in \emph{any} wall, get the
flow on it. Can the same verified cells compute the flow on a
prescribed wall?

\section{How it works, in full}

The engine keeps everything upstream of the wall unchanged --- the same
throat start, the same initial expansion fan --- and changes only how
each column of computed points ends. In the generator, the wall point
is \emph{produced}: the march integrates the mass-flow streamline and
the wall is wherever it goes. In the inverse mode the wall point's
\emph{position is given} (it must sit on the prescribed contour at that
station, with the contour's local slope supplied as data), so the wall
cell works backwards, exactly as described in Chapter~\ref{ch:moc}: it
finds the \emph{foot} of the arriving \emph{characteristic} --- the
point on the previous column from which the line carrying information
to the wall point set out --- by a chord search, scanning the stored
segments of that column for the one whose data brackets the
characteristic condition. It then closes the compatibility relation
along the chord from foot to wall point, with the prescribed wall slope
supplying the flow direction there~\cite{zucrow}. One march column is
built per prescribed wall station.

Two consequences shape the rest of the engine. First, an arbitrary wall
does not produce a uniform, axial exit, so nothing downstream may
assume one: thrust is always evaluated by the wall route
\eqref{eq:route2}, which needs only the wall pressures and the throat
flux. Second, the whole construction remains differentiable --- the
foot search, the chord closure, the thrust quadrature --- so
reverse-mode automatic differentiation supplies
$\mathrm{d}J/\mathrm{d}({\rm wall})$, the sensitivity of thrust to a
free-form wall change, at the cost of roughly one extra march.

That gradient is certified by the \emph{envelope identity}, which is
the fundamental theorem of calculus used as a test: the average of a
derivative over an interval must equal the secant slope across it,
$\overline{\mathrm{d}J/\mathrm{d}a} = [J(a_2)-J(a_1)]/(a_2-a_1)$. So
the test deforms the wall by a smooth localized bump of amplitude $a$,
sweeps $a$ over a range, fits a straight line to the AD gradient
samples, and requires the fitted mean slope to match a wide secant of
$J$ itself within a derived band. Why not the obvious pointwise
comparison of AD against a small-step finite difference? Because the
gas properties are read from tables interpolated piecewise-linearly, so
the code's exact derivative carries a microscopic sawtooth at the
table-cell scale, and a small-step difference straddles those kinks:
that test is ill-posed here. The finding and its diagnosis are in
Appendix~\ref{app:ripple}; the envelope certificate is its resolution
and Figure~\ref{fig:ripple} its one-picture summary.

\caption{Why the gradient is certified by envelope rather than

\section{What was built}

\carrier{validation/a1\_wall\_march.py}: the prescribed-wall engine
(\code{wall\_march}), the station extractor
(\code{stations\_from\_wall}), the wall-route thrust
(\code{J\_wall}), per-column mass flux, the envelope-identity
gradient certificate, and rejectors (a wall whose slope data is
corrupted must crash the march).

\section{Results of record}

\textbf{Twin stage, 5/5 PASS.} Fed Brick~1's own contour as a
prescribed wall, the new engine reproduces Brick~1's thrust through a
completely different computation --- $J_{\rm wall} = 1.25534\times
10^{8}$~N against $J_{\rm exit} = 1.25521\times10^{8}$~N, a difference
of $1.37\times10^{4}$ inside a derived band of $1.58\times10^{5}$. The
mass flow through the last computed column equals the throat mass flow
to 12.3~kg/s (band 152), an independent conservation check the twin
comparison does not contain; the minimum Mach number along the wall is
1.20, so the flow stays supersonic everywhere and the method remains
applicable; 17{,}221 cells are Newton-certified, i.e.\ every local
solve converged inside its derived residual band; and the
slope-corrupted wall crashes the march --- rejected.
\textbf{Gradient stage, 2/2 PASS} via the envelope identity:
$|{\rm AD} - {\rm secant}| = 29$ against a derived band of 109, and the
sensitivity is itself far larger than that band, so the wall
demonstrably matters.

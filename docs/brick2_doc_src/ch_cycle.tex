\chapter{The cycle layer: the engine becomes an RDE tool}
\label{ch:cycle}

Until now the nozzle had one operating point: one chamber pressure,
one chamber temperature, one design. A rotating detonation engine does
not offer one. This chapter teaches the optimizer to design against a
whole periodic cycle of chamber conditions --- and then proves it did
so correctly, by making it reproduce, blindly, two answers the theory
already knows and a shortcut-taking implementation could not find.

\section{Cycle-averaged optimization and its oracles}

\subsection{What you need to know first}

A \emph{rotating detonation engine} (RDE) burns its propellant in a
ring-shaped (annular) chamber around which one or more
\emph{detonation waves} --- supersonic combustion fronts, a shock with
the flame locked to it --- run continuously, typically a few thousand
times per second, fresh propellant refilling the gap behind each wave.
The consequence for the nozzle is the point of this chapter: a nozzle
cross-section never sees steady conditions. Once per revolution the
pressure jumps sharply as the wave sweeps past, then falls
(\emph{blows down}) until the next wave arrives.

What the nozzle responds to is the \emph{stagnation state}
$(P_0, T_0)$: the pressure and temperature the chamber gas would have
if brought smoothly to rest, the reservoir condition that fixes
everything downstream. In an RDE it is a periodic \emph{family}
$(P_0(\xi), T_0(\xi))$ indexed by the \emph{cycle phase}
$\xi \in [0,1]$, one full revolution of the wave; the \emph{phase
measure} $\mathrm{d}\mu(\xi)$ says how much of the cycle each phase
occupies, hence its weight in an average. And \emph{quasi-steady}
means the gas crosses the nozzle in far less time than the wave takes
to come round again --- so at each instant the flow is simply the
steady flow of that instant's chamber state. The design variable
throughout is the area ratio $\varepsilon = A_e/A_t$, exit area over
throat area (Chapter~\ref{ch:nozzle}).

\subsection{The question}

An RDE hands the nozzle a periodic \emph{family} of chamber states,
not one. Can the engine optimize the cycle-averaged thrust --- and
prove it does so correctly?

\subsection{How it works, in full}

Because the expansion is quasi-steady, the cycle-averaged thrust is
simply the phase average of steady thrusts:
\begin{equation}
J_{\rm cycle}(\varepsilon) \;=\; \int_0^1
F\!\left(\varepsilon;\, P_0(\xi),\, T_0(\xi)\right)\mathrm{d}\mu(\xi).
\label{eq:jcycle}
\end{equation}
The layer implements this \emph{honestly}: the integral is a
quadrature over a finite set of phases, and each phase gets its own
stagnation state built on the same gas tables, its own full march
through the nozzle, and its own thrust. No shortcut exploiting a
similarity between phases is coded anywhere --- deliberately, because
the theory's hardest test is about what an honest implementation must
then reproduce on its own.

That test is the collapse theorem for the fixed bell (its
T3)~\cite{theory}. It says: if only the pressure varies over the cycle
and the temperature does not (a \emph{pressure-only} blowdown, fixed
$T_0$), the whole cycle problem collapses to a classical steady design
at the mean pressure --- so cycle-aware optimization must buy
\emph{exactly nothing}. The reason is a similarity: at fixed $T_0$ and
fixed geometry, scaling $P_0$ scales every pressure in the nozzle by
the same factor and moves no wall and no Mach number, so
$J_{\rm cycle}$ is the steady $J$ at $\langle P_0\rangle$, exactly. An
engine marching each phase independently has no way to know this ---
unless it is correct, in which case the zero gain \emph{emerges}. That
emergence is the test.

The second stage poses the first mock RDE (user-posed): exponential
$P_0$ \emph{and} $T_0$ profiles in phase (peak-to-trough pressure
ratio $PR = 10$, temperature ratio $TR = 1.306$, peak 3850~K, five
phases spaced uniformly in the logarithm of pressure). With $T_0$
varying, the collapse is no longer exact, and the question becomes
quantitative: how close does the best \emph{one-number} design --- an
ordinary single-point design at one representative chamber state ---
come to the blind cycle optimum, and which state is the right one?
The theory's answer --- the mean pressure at the
\emph{pressure-weighted} temperature
$\langle P T\rangle/\langle P\rangle$ --- is tested against the
log-mean alternative in a derived comparison, and a design made at the
peak-pressure phase supplies the deliberately wrong reference.

Building a phase's stagnation state on tabulated real-gas
properties has traps of its own; the three that were met (and the
rejectors that now guard them) are Appendix~\ref{app:cycletraps}.

\subsection{Results of record}

\textbf{The zero-gain collapse oracle: 5/5 PASS}
(\carrier{validation/a1\_cycle\_layer.py}, stage \code{t3}). The
optimizer, told nothing about the theorem and spending five full
marches per objective evaluation, lands on the design-at-the-mean
within its window: the gain it finds over that design is $-475$~N out
of $1.16\times10^{8}$~N, against a derived band of
$9.6\times10^{3}$~N --- zero, to the precision the test can resolve.
The deliberately wrong peak-pressure design loses $4.8\times10^{6}$~N,
$500\times$ the band, so a real gain would have been seen. And the
similarity shows up live: at fixed geometry the five per-phase walls
coincide to $8.9\times10^{-16}$ --- pressure scaling does not move the
wall, measured at machine zero.

\textbf{The mock RDE: 6/6 PASS} (stage \code{mock}). The blind
optimum lands within $-0.74\%$ in $\varepsilon$ of the best
one-number design --- which is indeed mean pressure at the
pressure-weighted temperature $\langle PT\rangle/\langle P\rangle$,
beating the log-mean reference in a derived comparison (the theory's
weighted-average lesson, confirmed at engine level); the thrust
penalty of that one-number design is $2.5\times10^{-6}$ relative
(second order, as predicted), while the peak design loses 4\% of $J$.
The practical reading: for this class of cycle one well-chosen state
is enough, and the theory says which.

\section{The brokered compromise, on a meter}
\label{sec:votes}

\subsection{The question}

The theory's most quotable claim about RDE nozzles~\cite{theory} is
the averaged corner condition: ``no phase satisfies its own corner
condition; the cycle average does.'' Can that be measured?

\subsection{How it works, in full}

Recall the corner condition of Section~\ref{sec:corner}: the exact
statement that a nozzle's wall stops at the right place, whose leading
term is the mismatch between the pressure the gas reaches at the exit
and the ambient pressure it exhausts into. At the cycle optimum every
phase $\xi$ casts a \emph{vote} through that same meter:
its own exit-pressure mismatch $R(\xi) = p_e(\xi) - p_a$. A phase with
$R > 0$ leaves the nozzle still pushing harder than its surroundings
--- \emph{underexpanded}, voting ``lengthen me''. A phase with
$R < 0$ has expanded below ambient --- \emph{overexpanded}, voting
``shorten me''. The claim is that the optimum satisfies no phase
individually: it is a brokered compromise in which the
$\mu$-weighted mean of the votes is zero while each vote is not.

The meter reads the votes off the cached cycle runs, and every
threshold is derived. The balance band around zero comes from the
objective's own curvature and the width of the window where the
optimizer stopped --- how far from zero the mean could legitimately
sit. Each phase's vote must then individually exceed that band;
otherwise ``no phase satisfies its own condition'' would be an
assertion rather than a measurement.

\subsection{Results of record}

\textbf{8/8 PASS on both cycles}
(\carrier{validation/a1\_cycle\_corner.py}, an analysis of the cached
cycle runs --- 0.4~s). At the mock-RDE optimum the five phases' votes
run from $+7.9\times10^{5}$ down to $-5.1\times10^{5}$~Pa ---
monotone in phase, with exactly one sign change, each individually
2.8--3.2$\times$ the balance band and 14--90$\times$ the achieved
average --- while their \emph{mean} sits at zero within the derived
band. At the wrong (peak-pressure) design the meter pins at
$-4.6\times10^{5}$~Pa: every phase overexpanded, no compromise at
all. A wrong design is visible at a glance.

\chapter{Status, what remains, and reproduction}
\label{ch:status}

This chapter is the ledger. It lists every test suite the program
owns and its verdict, states exactly which quantities a design run
may fix and which it may move, says what is not yet built, and
gives the command that re-runs each result from scratch. Nothing
here is new physics: it is the part a reader needs in order to
check the rest.

\section{Design inputs and constraints, as of today}
\label{sec:designinputs}

\subsection{What a design run can fix}

Designing means holding some quantities fixed and letting the
optimizer move the rest. Every item below can be held fixed
independently for a run; whatever is not held is what the
optimization is allowed to change.

\emph{Gas and chamber}: the property tables (any gas model inside
their range of validity), the chamber conditions $(P_0, T_0)$ ---
the pressure and temperature the gas would have at rest --- and,
for RDE runs, the whole cycle $P_0(\xi), T_0(\xi)$ with the weight
given to each phase $\xi$ of the cycle.

\emph{Environment}: the ambient pressure $p_a$ the nozzle exhausts
into, vacuum included. For a plug nozzle it is not merely
bookkeeping: it enters the flow itself, since the free jet boundary
is the surface on which $p = p_a$.

\emph{Throat geometry}: the throat radius $y_t$ and the two arc
radii $r_{tu}, r_{td}$ that round the throat, all differentiable
knobs. Holding $y_t$ \emph{is} the mass-flow constraint: a choked
throat passes a fixed mass flow $\dot m$, so fixing its radius
fixes the engine's flow rate.

\emph{Overall size}: the area ratio $\varepsilon$ --- the exit area
divided by the throat area, the single number saying how much the
nozzle expands the gas (the one-knob driver of
Chapters~\ref{ch:firstopt} and~\ref{ch:cycle}).

\emph{The wall}: an output when the engine generates a contour; a
fully given input in the inverse bell and plug modes; and a
parameterized unknown when it is being optimized --- today small
bumps whose ends are pinned, spline control points next. Pinning
the endpoint \emph{is} the fixed-length constraint: it is exactly
the problem Rao posed, and exactly what the stationarity test of
Section~\ref{sec:o33} held fixed.

\emph{Modes}: planar or axisymmetric; a single steady operating
point or a cycle average. \emph{Resolution}: the number of fan
rays, stations and start rows --- not design variables, but fixed
per run and part of every derived error band.

\subsection{Which constraints can be held simultaneously}

On the JAX side, constraints are today imposed \emph{by
construction} rather than by a general constrained optimizer: mass
flow by holding the throat, length by pinning the lip, ambient as
posed data --- and the optimizer moves whatever is left (today
$\varepsilon$, or the wall through its bump basis). Holding several
nonlinear constraints at once against the AD gradient needs the
general machinery: a trust-region SQP driver --- a standard
constrained optimizer that takes a limited-size step each iteration
and enforces the constraints to first order --- which is on the
remaining-work list, and for which the certified prescribed-wall
paths were built.

On the GENO side, the RaoPlug module (its case type 8) accepts,
since the
two-constraint session, exactly \emph{two} simultaneous
constraints. The first is the \emph{mass constraint}, always on:
the design curve is terminated where the mass accumulated inside it
equals the throat's target $p_c\,\pi y_t^2/c^{*}$, and that
condition is consumed by the point $D$ at which the spike is cut.
The second is \emph{one} user-chosen design constraint, consumed by
a bisection on the exit angle $\theta_E$ --- area ratio, plug
length, exit angle, thrust, exit Mach number, ambient pressure at
the lip, or the base pressure $p_b$ (matched against the value the
corner condition implies at the mass-set cut, the meaningful
pairing with the mass constraint).

Two is not an implementation limit but a \emph{theoretical} one.
Rao's spike family has exactly two degrees of freedom at a fixed
lip radius --- the exit angle $\theta_E$ and the cut point $D$ ---
so two conditions exhaust it, and a third would be over-determined.
The one honest route to a third simultaneous constraint is to free
the lip radius $y_E$ as a scale degree of freedom, an outer
bisection already sketched in GENO's design notes and not yet
built.

\section{What remains, in order}

The GENO thread is now closed end to end. The owner's decision was
to solve the two-constraint problem, and the GENO session landed
it: the design curve terminates at the prescribed throat mass, the
cut point supplies the second degree of freedom with the corner
condition returning the base pressure it implies, four rigor
defects were fixed on the way, and three independent checks PASS
(the duality between the two ways of writing the endpoint
condition, $2\times10^{-5}$; the exact planar limit, bit for bit;
an independent Python integration of the design curve, three to
four digits). One item did not close: the numbers tabulated in the
1961 spike paper as the registry quotes them proved unverifiable
and internally inconsistent (GENO's N-74), so the owner must
procure the original paper. The full-field comparison --- our plug
march run on GENO's own spike --- is now certified 8/8 in
Chapter~\ref{ch:genotwin}, with the measured three-to-four-digit
difference between the two codes characterized and its cause filed
as an open question on GENO's side (Section~\ref{sec:twingap}).

The free-vortex swirl extension is certified at march level in
Chapter~\ref{ch:swirl}: the structural guarantee measured rather
than assumed, an exact duct solution reproduced, and the
switch-off regression at $10^{-13}$. Using it inside the plug and
cycle studies is now a posing choice, not new machinery. Beyond the
free vortex the flow is rotational and belongs to the CFD-adjoint
track.

The free-form wall, listed here for several sessions as the last
missing piece, is now built: Chapter~\ref{ch:spline} gives the spike a
cubic-spline basis, an adjoint gradient and a trust-region driver, and
the result is a contour the program computes rather than inherits. Two
things follow from how it was built. It uses the \emph{bell}
optimizer's spline routines directly rather than a basis of its own,
so bell and plug now carry the same shape freedom and the comparison
of Chapter~\ref{ch:compare} becomes an equal-terms one. And the
representation ladder of Section~\ref{sec:reprladder} shows the plug's
error concentrating in the single region where its contour bends,
which is an argument for adaptive knot placement rather than for more
knots.

\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{Resolve the free-form spike's gain} --- RESOLVED
  (\S\ref{sec:gainresolved}): on a six-rung paired ladder to
  $(1921,1601)$, with the incumbent re-optimized at $(121,101)$ and
  the model made to predict its last rung before seeing it, the gain
  limit is $-0.011\% \pm 0.017\%$ --- zero within the band. The
  truncated streamline is optimal at this length and inlet split to
  within $0.028\%$; every previously measured gain was instrument.
\item \textbf{Adaptive knot placement} on the spike --- DONE
  (\S\ref{sec:adaptive}); its verdict check is the previous item,
  now closed.
\item \textbf{Re-run the configuration comparison} with both sides
  free-form, now that the plug has a basis --- and only once the
  previous item is settled, since a margin quoted against an
  unresolved gain would inherit its problem.
\item \textbf{The Rao comparison at full expansion}: pose the
  classical construction and this optimizer at the SAME length
  ($5.825$ m) with mass AND ambient matched (\S\ref{sec:vsrao}). At
  $2.5$ m the two codes describe different machines and their thrusts
  are not comparable.
\item \textbf{The plug's $1\%$ momentum residual}
  (\S\ref{sec:fmeter}): measured now on a good surface and surviving
  refinement, so it is the branch's outstanding physical question.
\item \textbf{The control surface in the O3.3 bench}: our terminal
  characteristic is taken as a whole grid column, selected by
  minimising the very spread the check then reports. The measured
  spread ($4.0\times10^{-3}$) shows no pathology, but the locus should
  be fixed \emph{a priori} and stopped at the kernel boundary rather
  than chosen by the statistic it feeds.
\item \textbf{Measured-cycle input}: a data swap --- mind the
  table-range rejector of Chapter~\ref{ch:cycle}.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Which solver these numbers stand on}
\label{sec:rebase}

Every result in this document was originally produced on one version
of the underlying flow solver, and has since been re-run on another.
The solver's Newton loop was changed independently: where it once took
a fixed number of iterations per cell, it now stops when the step
falls below the cell's own certification bound. That is a real change
to how the arithmetic terminates, and it sits underneath every number
here, so it was not assumed harmless.

It was measured instead. Re-running the case that fixes the common
basis on both versions gives mass flow agreeing \emph{bit for bit},
and thrust, exit Mach, exit speed, exit pressure and lip radius
agreeing to between $10^{-15}$ and $10^{-13}$ relative --- machine
precision, across four area ratios. The interpretation is
straightforward: the fixed-iteration loop was already converging past
the point where the new stopping rule halts, so the two agree to the
last digit either can carry.

No number in this document moves as a result. Every suite listed in
Table~\ref{tab:status} was re-run on the new solver and returned its
recorded verdict.

\section{Reproduction}

Each carrier script is self-contained and prints its own verdict;
runs are cached where noted, so re-runs are cheap. On host
\code{s2}:

\begin{lstlisting}
cd RDE_Design-rde-nozzle-program
.venv-a1/bin/python validation/a1_ideal_march_jax.py    # Brick 1
.venv-a1/bin/python validation/a1_thrust_functional.py  # the score and the compass
.venv-a1/bin/python validation/a1_corner_instrument.py  # the score and the compass

Two of these need data the scripts do not generate themselves. The
GENO plug twin reads a cached field from the independent Fortran code
(\code{validation/\_geno\_twin/}), and the O3.3 bench reads a designed
contour from the same source. Both are products of a code this
document does not modify, so they are inputs rather than results. The
O3.3 bench also carries a second stage that the default invocation
does not run:

\begin{lstlisting}
STAGE=stationarity .venv-a1/bin/python validation/a1_o33_toc.py
\end{lstlisting}

\code{docs/brick2\_doc\_src/}: \code{make\_figures.py} regenerates
the data figures from the cached run data, and \code{build.sh}
compiles \code{main.tex} (Tectonic engine) and installs the PDF as
\code{docs/rde\_nozzle\_A1\_brick2\_thrust.pdf}.

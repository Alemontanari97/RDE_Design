\chapter{The first optimization, against a known answer}
\label{ch:firstopt}

The previous chapter gave the engine a score and a compass. This one
closes the loop for the first time: a search that proposes designs,
scores them with the march, and keeps going until it cannot improve.
The point is not the answer but the audit --- the problem is posed so
small that its solution is known in advance on paper, so the machine
can be judged against it. A loop that lands on a known answer while
knowing nothing about it can be trusted on problems whose answer is not
known.

\section{The question}

Does the loop close? Propose, score, search --- and land where theory
says the optimum is?

\section{How it works, in full}

The first optimization is deliberately one-dimensional, so that its
answer exists in closed form (an exact formula, as opposed to a number
obtained by iteration). The single knob is the \emph{area ratio}
$\varepsilon = A_e/A_t$ --- the exit area divided by the throat area,
the one number that says how much the nozzle lets the gas expand
(Chapter~\ref{ch:nozzle}). Everything else is held fixed.

The known answer comes from the exit-area lemma. Differentiate the
thrust~\eqref{eq:route1} with respect to the exit area of an ideal
(uniform-exit) nozzle: enlarging the exit by $\mathrm{d}A_e$ adds a
ring of wall on which the local exit pressure $p_e$ pushes forward,
while the ambient pressure $p_a$ pushes back on the same ring, so
\begin{equation}
\frac{\mathrm{d}J}{\mathrm{d}A_e} \;=\; p_e - p_a
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
\text{optimum exactly where } p_e(\varepsilon^{*}) = p_a .
\label{eq:arealemma}
\end{equation}
A nozzle whose gas is still above ambient at the exit ($p_e > p_a$) is
called \emph{underexpanded}: it has not finished expanding, and is
worth lengthening. Once the gas has been expanded below ambient
($p_e < p_a$) the nozzle is \emph{overexpanded}: the surroundings now
push back harder than the gas pushes forward on each new ring, and
every extra ring costs thrust. The optimum is the crossover. Posing an
ambient pressure therefore fixes $\varepsilon^{*}$ analytically --- the
march only has to be inverted for the area ratio at which its exit
pressure equals that ambient.

The search itself knows none of this. It is a \emph{golden-section
search}: a derivative-free method that keeps a bracket $[a,b]$ known to
contain the maximum, evaluates the objective at two interior points
placed by the golden ratio, discards the end that cannot hold the
maximum, and repeats until the bracket is narrower than a set
tolerance. Each shrink reuses one of the two previous evaluations,
which is what the golden ratio buys. Its only inputs are calls to the
march and readings of $J$ --- no gradient, no knowledge of the lemma.
Every march evaluation is cached to disk keyed by $\varepsilon$, so an
interrupted run resumes at no cost, and the landscape in
Figure~\ref{fig:jeps} is drawn from the search's own evaluations rather
than from a separate sweep.

The suite then closes three independent loops around the answer. The
area ratio found by the blind search must match the closed form. The
exit pressure there must equal the posed ambient, within a band derived
from the march's own two-resolution error estimate plus the width of
the bracket the search stopped on. And the corner meter of
Section~\ref{sec:corner} --- built two steps earlier, never consulted
by the search --- must independently read zero at the design that was
found.

\section{What was built}

\carrier{validation/a1\_driver\_eps.py}: the golden-section driver,
the disk cache, the three closure checks, and a rejector --- a
deliberately corrupted objective, with the pressure--area term
$(p_e-p_a)A_e$ deleted, which must then run monotonically to the edge
of the bracket instead of finding an interior optimum, because without
that term nothing ever penalizes over-expansion.

\section{Results of record}

\textbf{4/4 PASS} (231~s, 13 march evaluations). The blind search lands
at $\varepsilon = 5.1551$ against the analytic $5.1506$; the exit
pressure at the found optimum matches the posed ambient to 916~Pa,
inside a derived band of 7958~Pa; the corner meter independently reads
$\approx 0$ there, so the optimality condition of Rao's theory is
satisfied at a design the search reached without ever evaluating it;
and the corrupted objective runs to the bracket edge --- rejected.

\begin{abstract}
\noindent
This document records the construction of a certified nozzle
\emph{optimizer} for rotating detonation engines. Its starting
point is a flow solver, delivered earlier, that computes the
supersonic flow through a given nozzle and --- because it is
written in a differentiable language --- also returns the exact
derivative of any computed result with respect to any input, at
roughly the cost of one extra solve. This report is the climb from
that solver to an optimizer: a thrust objective computed by two
independent routes, its gradient, a classical optimality condition
turned into a working instrument, a first blind optimization
checked against a closed-form answer, the structural inversion that
makes the wall shape an input rather than an output, an independent
Fortran code brought in as referee, a term-by-term confrontation of
Rao's 1958 hand-derived optimality conditions with the automatic
adjoint on a real thrust-optimized contour, the cycle layer that
makes the engine an RDE tool, the plug (aerospike) march with its
free exhaust boundary, the question of where to cut a truncated
plug when the chamber pressure swings, a full-field confrontation
with the independent code on its own designed spike, and the
extension to swirling exhaust.

Every step carries its own certified test suite. Every tolerance is
derived --- typically by the computation measuring its own
discretization error --- rather than chosen; every suite contains
deliberate corruptions that it must catch. All numbers quoted are
results of record from actual runs.

The first three chapters assume no background whatsoever: what a
nozzle is and why it has an optimum, how the solver computes, and
how this program decides that something is true. The engine
chapters follow, then a status ledger; every problem met on the way
--- the bugs the tests caught, with diagnosis and fix --- is
collected in the appendix.
\end{abstract}
